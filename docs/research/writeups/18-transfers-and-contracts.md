# Transfers and contracts: knowledge time and no causal overreach

## Question

Can we associate transfers and contract horizons with outcomes without letting a
later announcement explain an earlier forecast, and without claiming to know private
negotiations?

## Knowledge time

A transfer or contract fact has two dates: when it applied (registration, contract
start) and when it became public. A model building features at a prediction origin
may use only facts whose known date is at or before the origin
(`economics.transfers`). A move announced after the origin is retrospective and is
labelled as such; it cannot explain the earlier forecast.

## What stays unknown

Undisclosed fees, clauses, wages, and the content of negotiations are not public.
Fees are modelled as disclosed intervals, free, or explicitly undisclosed, never
guessed. The evidence ledger requires a source citation before a manual record
enters the modelling set (`economics.evidence`).

## Causal caution

Transfer adaptation (does output settle toward a prior level after a move) and
contract-horizon associations are predictive questions with censoring and competing
events, not causal claims about why a club acted. The writeups label them
predictive, and the contract horizon model would use survival-style censoring.

## Evidence

Tests confirm a move announced after a forecast origin is treated as retrospective
and blocked, fee intervals and unknowns behave correctly, squad continuity is
computed from registration spells, and the evidence ledger requires a source.

## Conclusion

The economics domain respects knowledge time, keeps unknowns unknown, and frames
transfer and contract questions as predictive associations with their censoring and
confounders stated.
